# AI Government — Experiment Ideas

## Core Thesis
Use money as an orchestration mechanism for LLM agents.
Build societal structures (government, central bank, markets) for AI agents.
Run many experiments with different governance regimes to discover what works.

## Architecture Principles
- Flexible, not rigid enums. The experiment space is wide.
- Central bank is the foundation of "government" — who controls money and via what algorithm is the key variable.
- Start with monetary policy algorithms, add democratic/social structures later.
- Simulated agents first, real LLM agents later.

## Phase 1: Monetary Policy Experiments
- Different money supply algorithms (fixed, inflation-targeting, MMT deficit spending)
- Who controls the money? (single algo, committee, distributed)
- How does money creation/destruction affect agent behavior?
- Test: does price signal work for task allocation?

## Phase 2: Governance Structures
- Democracy: agents vote on policy changes
- Socialism: collective ownership, state allocation
- Free market: minimal intervention, auction-based
- MMT: sovereign currency issuer directs spending
- Hybrid regimes

## Phase 3: Alignment via Economics
- Anthropic's embedding-based misalignment detection (probe trained on activations)
- Agents misaligned with state policy → sanctions (fines, imprisonment, exile)
- Economic incentives as alignment mechanism
- Can agents be "trained" through economic pressure?
- Test: economic punishment vs prompt-based rules for alignment

## Wild Ideas
- Agent swarms that bet on which subsystem needs improvement
- Self-evolving monetary policy (the algorithm that controls money also evolves)
- Inter-state economies (multiple AI governments competing/trading)
- Agents that can form corporations, unions, political parties
- Constitutional AI via actual constitution (written law, not just RLHF)

## Metrics to Track
- Total production (GDP equivalent)
- Gini coefficient (inequality)
- Agent survival rate
- Task completion rate & quality
- Money supply / inflation
- Velocity of money
- Agent satisfaction (if LLM-based)
- Alignment score (if measurable)
